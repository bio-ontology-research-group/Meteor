Salvage-pathway gene presence check (Salmonella, dpz), read-only cross-reference
of feature-table product annotations against saved sgd_salmonella_{meteor,thresh}_dpz.json.

Salvage CATALYTIC enzymes (hpt/hypoxanthine-PRTase, gpt/xanthine-PRTase,
apt/adenine-PRTase, upp/uracil-PRTase, deoD/purine-nucleoside-phosphorylase)
are present with a GPR-assigned reaction in BOTH METEOR's and threshold's
selected networks identically (in_METEOR=True, in_THRESH=True for all 6
hits checked) -- ruling out simple 'salvage enzyme gene missing from
METEOR' as the explanation.

Salvage TRANSPORTERS (nucleoside permeases, NupC, PunC, xanthine/uracil
permease, GhxP) are absent from BOTH models identically (in_METEOR=False,
in_THRESH=False for all 11 transporter-like hits checked) -- so transporter
absence, if relevant, applies equally to both arms and cannot itself explain
the meteor-vs-threshold difference either.

Conclusion: the differentiator is NOT simple gene/reaction presence-absence
for the salvage pathway itself. It must lie in something not directly
observable from the saved per-gene calls -- e.g. whether the salvage route
is actually WIRED to carry flux to biomass under FBA in each arm's specific
selected network (could differ even with identical gene-level GPR presence,
depending on which OTHER connecting reactions/exchanges got selected), which
requires the actual y-vector (selected reaction set) per arm to trace
mechanically. NOT resolvable from saved data alone -- flagged as the
concrete limit on point 3's finer mechanism distinction.
