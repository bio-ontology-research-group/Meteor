Verification of E. coli essentiality-reference medium provenance (2026-09-17).
Sources: PubMed full text via MCP tool (According to PubMed), PEC database
(https://shigen.nig.ac.jp/ecoli/pec/), direct fetch.

1. Baba T et al. "Construction of Escherichia coli K-12 in-frame, single-gene
   knockout mutants: the Keio collection." Mol Syst Biol 2006;2:2006.0008.
   PMID 16738554, PMC1681482, DOI 10.1038/msb4100050.

   Verbatim, Methods ("Construction and verification of deletion mutants"):
   "Our standard protocol usually yielded 10-1000 Km^r colonies when cells
   were incubated aerobically at 37C on Luria broth (LB) agar containing
   30 ug/ml kanamycin."
   -> primary viability/essentiality screen = LB (rich) agar. 303 genes
      "we were unable to disrupt ... candidates for essential genes"
      (abstract) -- this is an LB-based, condition-independent criterion:
      failure to construct a viable deletion AT ALL.

   Separately (Results, "Profiling contributions of individual genes during
   growth on rich and minimal media"):
   "All mutants were profiled for growth yield in both rich (LB) and
   minimal glucose MOPS media."
   -> this is a SEPARATE, secondary quantitative fitness comparison of the
      already-LB-viable (non-essential) mutants; it does not redefine
      "essential," it characterizes conditional growth differences among
      genes that already passed the LB viability bar.

2. Yamamoto N et al. "Update on the Keio collection of Escherichia coli
   single-gene deletion mutants." Mol Syst Biol 2009;5:335. PMID 20029369,
   PMC2824493, DOI 10.1038/msb.2009.92.

   Verbatim: "...some ORFs reported as essential in the PEC database are
   nonessential, at least not in the genetic background of our host E. coli
   K-12 BW25113 during aerobic growth at 37C on LB agar."
   -> confirms PEC and Keio share the same LB-agar-based essentiality
      criterion, and that this is explicitly the comparison condition used
      to validate/update the Keio essential-gene candidate list.

3. Monk JM et al. "iML1515, a knowledgebase that computes Escherichia coli
   traits." Nat Biotechnol 2017;35(10):904-908. PMID 29020004, PMC6521705,
   DOI 10.1038/nbt.3956. (= gapseq paper's ref 107, the citation gess-ecol.csv
   traces to.)

   Verbatim: "[Biolog-style growth] screens for the entire KEIO collection
   (3,892 gene knockouts) grown on 16 different carbon sources... We
   identified 345 genes that were essential in at least 1 of 16 conditions.
   188 genes were essential in all conditions, and 157 were essential in
   specific conditions. The iML1515 knowledgebase predicted gene
   essentiality in 16 conditions with an accuracy of 93.4%..."
   -> this is a DIFFERENT, condition-specific (M9-type minimal, varying
      carbon source) essentiality dataset, run only on the subset of genes
      that survived LB construction (i.e., built ON TOP of Keio's LB-based
      list, not instead of it).

CONCLUSION for Q1: layered, not a single answer.
  - The classic Keio/PEC "essential" bar (the ~287-303-gene list) is
    LB/rich-medium-based: "could not construct a viable deletion at all."
  - Monk 2017 (gapseq's own cited source) ran an ADDITIONAL M9-carbon-source
    screen on the (LB-viable) remainder, producing condition-specific
    essentiality (188-345 genes) -- this is likely closer to what
    gess-ecol.csv's "ess.experimental" reflects, since gapseq cites Monk
    2017 specifically, and gess-ecol.csv's count (234 of 1355 rows marked
    essential, restricted to genes present in the compared GEMs) does not
    match Baba's ~303 or PEC's 287 (whole-genome), nor Monk's 188/345
    (whole 3892-gene Keio-viable set) exactly -- consistent with a further
    GEM-gene-only restriction of one of these lists, most plausibly Monk's
    M9-based data given the citation chain, but this was not opened/verified
    row-by-row (gess-ecol.csv itself carries no provenance column).
  - So: gapseq's GS_MM_glc choice for scoring the RECONSTRUCTED MODEL likely
    DOES match the medium of the specific experimental dataset it cites
    (Monk 2017, M9/carbon-source screens) -- this is probably NOT a
    mismatch, contrary to the initial suspicion. It is the PEC/Baba
    LB-based list (a DIFFERENT, stricter, condition-independent
    essentiality bar) that would give the "genuinely rich-medium" E. coli
    comparison the user wants for the LB-vs-LB, M9-vs-M9 test.

4. PEC (Profiling of E. coli Chromosome), https://shigen.nig.ac.jp/ecoli/pec/
   Direct download, NO gate: https://shigen.nig.ac.jp/ecoli/pec/download/files/PECData.dat
   (tab-separated, 4497 rows). Own methodology page (about.jsp), verbatim:
   "when a strain having only a null type mutation in a gene (without other
   suppressor mutations) was able to grow, even if the mutation meant that
   the strain could grow only at a certain temperature or under certain
   nutrient conditions, it is classified into the 'non-essential' category."
   -> PEC's "essential" class = fails to grow under EVERY documented
      condition across the literature it aggregates (condition-independent,
      strictest bar) -- for the large majority of these genes the founding
      evidence is literally "could not be disrupted on LB" (Keio), per-gene
      PMID citations in the file trace back to Keio/related disruption
      papers. Saved to ref/pec/PECData.dat (this directory).
      287 essential / 4029 non-essential / 3 unknown among 4319
      protein-coding-gene rows; genome-referenced to MG1655 (PEC's stated
      base strain), b-numbers given directly in the "Alternative name"
      column (e.g. b0001).
